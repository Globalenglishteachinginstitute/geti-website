Learning objective: Understand how American English sounds differ from letters and learn to read IPA.

1. Core concept and detailed rules

Read each explanation carefully before practicing the sounds and examples. General American is a useful instructional reference, not the only correct American variety.

  1. Spelling and pronunciation are not identical: “though,” “through,” and “tough” share letters but have different vowels and consonants.
  2. Most General American varieties are rhotic: the /r/ sound is pronounced in words such as car and hard.
  3. Use IPA between slashes / / for phonemes. Stress marks (ˈ) show the syllable that receives primary emphasis.
  4. A phoneme is a contrastive sound: changing /ɪ/ in ship to /i/ creates sheep.
  5. Make recordings and compare a single feature at a time rather than guessing from spelling.

Read each explanation carefully before practicing the sounds and examples. General American is a useful instructional reference, not the only correct American variety.

  1. Vowel quality depends on tongue height, tongue position, lip rounding and tenseness.
